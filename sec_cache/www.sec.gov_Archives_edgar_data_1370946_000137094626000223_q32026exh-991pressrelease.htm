Owens Corning Announces Third-Quarter 2026 Results
TOLEDO, Ohio – November 4, 2026 - Owens Corning (NYSE: OC), a branded building products leader, today reported third-quarter 2026 results.
•Reported Net Sales of $2.5 Billion, a 7% Decrease from Prior Year
•Generated Net Earnings Margin of (10)% and Adjusted EBITDA Margin of 20%
•Delivered Diluted EPS of $(3.15) and Adjusted Diluted EPS of $2.44, which excludes the impact of Non-Cash, Pre-Tax Impairment Charges of $476 Million related to the Doors Business
•Produced Operating Cash Flow of $813 Million and Free Cash Flow of $622 Million
•Returned $337 Million to Shareholders through Dividends and Share Repurchases; Reduced Debt by $299 Million
“During the quarter, challenging residential market conditions were further impacted by a second consecutive year of weak storm activity and rapidly rising transportation and input material costs. Despite these pressures, our team demonstrated disciplined commercial and operational execution, delivering a resilient margin and strong cash flow,” said Chair and Chief Executive Officer Brian Chambers. “Our performance reflects the fundamentally stronger company we have built, and we remain focused on executing our strategy and leveraging our unique OC AdvantagesTM to create additional long-term value for our customers and shareholders.”

Enterprise Highlights
•Owens Corning maintained a high level of safety performance with a recordable incident rate (RIR) of 0.81.

•Todd Fister was promoted to President and Chief Operating Officer, leading efforts to unlock efficiencies, strengthen execution, and accelerate organic growth across the enterprise. Jonathan Collins joined Owens Corning in August as Executive Vice President and Chief Financial Officer, succeeding Fister and bringing deep financial leadership experience and a proven background in scaling rapidly evolving businesses.

•Owens Corning placed second on the 100 Best Corporate Citizens list, marking the company's ninth consecutive year among the top 10. The list recognizes outstanding corporate responsibility performance among the largest publicly traded companies in the U.S.

Cash Returned to Shareholders
•In the third quarter, Owens Corning returned $337 million to shareholders. The company repurchased 1.9 million shares of common stock for $275 million and paid a quarterly cash dividend of $62 million. The company also reduced debt by $299 million and maintained net leverage within the targeted range of 2x to 3x.
“I am excited to partner with Brian and the leadership team as we build on Owens Corning’s strong foundation and invest in the long-term growth of the company,” said Executive Vice President and Chief Financial Officer Jonathan Collins. “In the third quarter, we continued to demonstrate our ability to generate attractive cash flow and returned significant capital to shareholders. Looking ahead, our financial approach remains grounded in disciplined capital allocation, maintaining a strong balance sheet, and preserving the financial flexibility to advance our strategic priorities.”

Third-Quarter Business Performance
•The company continued to deliver resilient margin performance. For the third quarter, Owens Corning generated an enterprise adjusted EBITDA margin of 20%.

Fourth-Quarter Outlook
•The key economic factors that impact the company’s business are residential repair activity, residential remodeling activity, U.S. housing starts, and commercial construction activity.
•Owens Corning expects existing home sales, discretionary remodeling activity, and residential new construction to remain under pressure. In Roofing, a second consecutive year without significant storm activity and related distribution destocking are expected to weigh on near-term demand. Non-residential construction in North America is expected to continue to show pockets of strength, and conditions in the company's core European markets are anticipated to remain stable.

•Owens Corning anticipates significant input and delivery cost inflation to result in incremental expense, with minimal price expectations in the quarter.

•For the fourth-quarter 2026, Owens Corning expects revenue of $2.0 billion to $2.2 billion. The company expects to generate an enterprise adjusted EBITDA margin of approximately 10% to 12%. This reflects the impacts of significantly higher inflation and transportation cost increases, as well as fourth-quarter seasonality and incremental downtime due to weaker market volumes.

“While we are navigating a challenging market in the near-term, we have confidence in OC’s long-term growth and performance through the cycle,” said President and Chief Operating Officer Todd Fister. “Actions we are taking today to further improve our cost position, strengthen our commercial capabilities, and increase the earnings potential of the company are positioning OC to create significant value over time.”

About Owens Corning
Owens Corning is a branded building products leader with three complementary market‑leading businesses providing roofing, insulation, and doors primarily for residential markets in North America and Europe. The company operates with an integrated go‑to‑market strategy and a unique set of OC Advantages™ – including its iconic brand, unparalleled commercial strength, leading technology, and winning cost position – to help customers win and grow in the market. Owens Corning is committed to helping build better and achieve more through winning partnerships, leading performance, and engaging people. Founded in 1938 and headquartered in Toledo, Ohio, Owens Corning is listed on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE: OC). For more information, visit www.owenscorning.com.

Use of Non-GAAP Measures
Owens Corning uses non-GAAP measures in its earnings press release that are intended to supplement investors' understanding of the company's financial information. These non-GAAP measures include EBITDA from continuing operations, adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations, adjusted earnings from continuing operations, adjusted diluted earnings per share attributable to Owens Corning common stockholders ("adjusted EPS") from continuing operations and free cash flow. When used to report historical financial information, reconciliations of these non-GAAP measures to the corresponding GAAP measures are included in the financial tables of this press release. Specifically, see Table 2 for adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations, Table 3 for adjusted earnings from continuing operations and adjusted EPS from continuing operations, and Table 8 for free cash flow.
For purposes of internal review of Owens Corning's year-over-year operational performance, management excludes from net earnings attributable to Owens Corning certain items it believes are not representative of ongoing operations. The non-GAAP financial measures resulting from these adjustments (including adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations, adjusted earnings from continuing operations and adjusted EPS from continuing operations) are used internally by Owens Corning for various purposes, including reporting results of operations to the Board of Directors, analysis of performance, and related employee compensation measures. Management believes that these adjustments result in a measure that provides a useful representation of its operational performance; however, the adjusted measures should not be considered in isolation or as a substitute for net earnings attributable to Owens Corning as prepared in accordance with GAAP.

Net debt and net leverage are non-GAAP financial measures that management uses to assess liquidity, financial flexibility and leverage. The company defines net debt as total debt less cash and cash equivalents. The company defines net leverage as net debt divided by adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations for the last twelve months ("LTM"). LTM represents the results for the twelve-month period ended as of the applicable balance sheet date. Free cash flow is a non-GAAP liquidity measure used by investors, financial analysts and management to help evaluate the company's ability to generate cash to pursue opportunities that enhance shareholder value. The company defines free cash flow as net cash flow provided by operating activities, less cash paid for property, plant and equipment. Free cash flow is not a measure of residual cash flow available for discretionary expenditures due to the company's mandatory debt service requirements. Free cash flow is used internally by the company for various purposes, including reporting results of operations to the Board of Directors of the company and analysis of performance.

Management believes that these measures provide a useful representation of our operational performance and liquidity; however, the measures should not be considered in isolation or as a substitute for net cash flow provided by operating activities or net earnings attributable to Owens Corning as prepared in accordance with GAAP.

When the company provides forward-looking expectations for non-GAAP measures, the most comparable GAAP measures and a reconciliation between the non-GAAP expectations and the corresponding GAAP measures are generally not available without unreasonable effort due to the variability, complexity and limited visibility of the adjusting items that would be excluded from the non-GAAP measures in future periods. The variability in timing and amount of adjusting items could have significant and unpredictable effect on our future GAAP results.
