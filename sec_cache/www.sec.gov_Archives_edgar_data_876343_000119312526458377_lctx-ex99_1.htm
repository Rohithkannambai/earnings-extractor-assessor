LINEAGE CELL THERAPEUTICS REPORTS THIRD QUARTER 2026 FINANCIAL RESULTS AND PROVIDES BUSINESS UPDATE

CARLSBAD, CA – November 12, 2026 - Lineage Cell Therapeutics, Inc. (NYSE American and TASE: LCTX), a clinical-stage biotechnology company developing novel, allogeneic, “off the shelf”, cell therapies for serious medical conditions, today reported its third quarter 2026 financial and operating results. The Company will host a conference call today at 4:30 p.m. Eastern Time to discuss these results and to provide a business update.

 

“While our collaboration with Roche and Genentech for the development of the OpRegen cell therapy program for dry AMD with geographic atrophy continues to advance, we are focused on using our platform to build a diversified portfolio of additional allogeneic cell transplant programs,” stated Brian M. Culley, Lineage CEO. “Today, we closed the acquisition of NeuroAirmid Therapeutics adding HUNT1, a third clinical-stage asset to our development pipeline. HUNT1 is a pluripotent stem cell derived, clinical-stage neural cell therapy program, with the potential to validate a new cell-based treatment modality for Huntington’s Disease. When we evaluated this opportunity, what stood out was the substantial development work that had been completed, external capital had been invested, and we believed our manufacturing expertise could create significant additional value for this program. This transaction reflects the disciplined approach to capital allocation and strategic fit that we believe our investors have come to expect from Lineage. HUNT1 was created on the same proprietary cell line that Lineage uses and is compatible with its AlloSCOPE “5D” technology, providing us with a head start on our planned contributions. Leveraging our experience with this line and our capabilities in cell manufacturing, process development, and scale-up, we intend to deploy our resources to migrate the HUNT1 process to our AlloSCOPE manufacturing platform and to support the evolution of HUNT1 onto a commercially viable and scalable production system more suitable to supporting late-stage registrational trials in parallel with its ongoing clinical development. In addition, the ongoing REGEN4HD clinical trial of HUNT1 is supported in part by a CIRM grant, reducing our out-of-pocket development costs.”

 

Mr. Culley continued, “In addition to this exciting acquisition, we also strengthened our proprietary manufacturing position through the issuance of a U.S. patent covering our AlloSCOPE 5D technology. 5D is an approach which we believe may provide a differentiated solution to one of the most important challenges facing the allogeneic cell therapy industry: enabling commercially-scalable and consistent manufacturing of pluripotent cell-derived therapies. We recently deployed AlloSCOPE 5D to COR1, our wholly owned corneal endothelial cell therapy program, and expect initial preclinical data in the first quarter of 2027, demonstrating the speed, scalability, and

 

 

 

versatility of our development platform. We also continued to employ 5D technology to advance our ILT1 program, which ultimately may become a treatment for Type 1 Diabetes.”

 

“In parallel with supporting the development of the OpRegen cell therapy program through the production, release, and delivery of multiple GMP batches of clinical trial material, we are prioritizing other programs that we believe can generate meaningful data in early clinical trials, and which supports our plan to optimize capital allocation and accelerate value creation. By combining strategic business development, proprietary manufacturing technologies, and internally generated innovation, we believe we are building a differentiated pipeline and positioning Lineage to create sustainable long-term value for our shareholders.” concluded Mr. Culley.

About the AlloSCOPE (Allogeneic, Scalable, Consistent, Off-the-shelf, Pluripotent Cell Engineering) Platform

The AlloSCOPE (Allogeneic, Scalable, Consistent, Off-the-shelf, Pluripotent Cell Engineering) platform highlights the key attributes of Lineage’s in-house technology and describes a differentiation and production modality from which Lineage can manufacture millions of doses of an allogeneic, cell-based product derived from a single initial pluripotent cell line, conferring consistent, cost-effective, and scalable cell-based production and which can be applied across multiple programs. From our proprietary AlloSCOPE platform, we successfully completed a current Good Manufacturing Practice (“cGMP”) production run from a custom, two-tiered cell banking system, featuring a genetically-stable master cell bank (MCB) created from a single, well-characterized pluripotent cell line, which generated a working cell bank (WCB), which then provided the source material for two final cell-based product candidates. AlloSCOPE “5D” describes an application of AlloSCOPE with the goal of higher scale production with reduced manipulation.

 

Allogeneic cell therapy product manufacturing generally involves three basic steps: (i) expansion of pluripotent cells; (ii) differentiation of cells into the desired cell type, and (iii) further expanding those cells prior to harvesting, filling and cryopreserving the final product. In cell therapy manufacturing, how cells are grown and organized during differentiation and/or expansion can affect their scalability, phenotype, process control, manufacturing cost, and the characteristics of the final product. In a 2D process, cells are typically grown as an adherent monolayer on a flat, coated surface, such as a culture flask or multilayer vessel. This method allows for close control of cell growth and morphology, but scaling generally requires increasing the number of culture vessels, which can add labor, facility space, cost, and process complexity. In a 3D process, cells are grown in a three-dimensional environment, often as aggregates or spheroids within a bioreactor. This method can support automation, higher cell densities, and greater scale from a smaller footprint, but requires a tradeoff of less control and loss of synchronization. Lineage’s AlloSCOPE 5D technology is the Company’s innovative process of merging 2D manufacturing with 3D scale. By creating 2D-like growth conditions within a controlled, scalable 3D environment, AlloSCOPE 5D is designed to address one of the central manufacturing challenges in large-scale cell therapy manufacturing, by harnessing the precision and uniformity advantages of 2D culture with the scalability, efficiency, and automation capabilities of 3D bioreactor systems, while simultaneously avoiding the scale limitations of 2D methods and unresolved challenges associated with aggregate-based 3D culture.

 

About Lineage Cell Therapeutics, Inc.

Lineage Cell Therapeutics is a clinical-stage biotechnology company developing novel allogeneic, or “off the shelf”, cell therapies for serious medical conditions. Lineage’s programs are based on its proprietary cell-based technology platform, AlloSCOPE™ (Allogeneic, Scalable, Consistent, Off-the-shelf, Pluripotent Cell Engineering), and associated development and manufacturing capabilities. From this proprietary AlloSCOPE platform, Lineage develops, manufactures, and tests specialized human cells with anatomical and physiological functions similar or substantially identical to cells found naturally in the human body. These cells are created by applying directed differentiation protocols to established, well-characterized, and self-renewing pluripotent cell lines. These protocols generate cells with characteristics associated with specific and desired developmental lineages, and in some instances may be designed to have additional beneficial properties. Cells derived from such

 

 

 

lineages are transplanted into patients in an effort to replace or support cells that are absent or dysfunctional due to degenerative disease, aging, or traumatic injury, and to restore or augment the patient’s functional activity. Lineage’s pipeline currently includes: (i) OpRegen® cell therapy, a retinal pigment epithelial cell therapy in Phase 2a development under a worldwide collaboration with Roche and Genentech, a member of the Roche Group, for the treatment of geographic atrophy secondary to age-related macular degeneration; (ii) HUNT1, a pluripotent stem cell derived, neural cell therapy in Phase 1b/2a development for the treatment of Huntington’s Disease; (iii) OPC1, an oligodendrocyte progenitor cell therapy in Phase 1/2a development for the treatment of spinal cord injuries; (iv) COR1, a corneal endothelial disease cell therapy in preclinical development for the potential treatment of corneal endothelial disease; (v) ReSonance® (ANP1), an auditory neuronal progenitor cell therapy in preclinical development under a collaboration with William Demant Invest A/S for the potential treatment of auditory neuropathy; (vi) ILT1, an islet cell therapy focused on the issue of large-scale production of undifferentiated pluripotent cells, which if successful and applied to an islet cell differentiation protocol could be evaluated for the production of islet cells for the potential treatment of Type 1 Diabetes; (vii) RND1, a novel hypoimmune induced pluripotent stem cell line being evaluated for development under a gene editing partnership; and (viii) PNC1, a photoreceptor neural cell therapy research initiative being evaluated for development for the potential treatment of vision loss due to photoreceptor dysfunction or damage.
